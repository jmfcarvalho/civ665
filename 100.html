MODULE 01 — GEOLOGICAL ENRICHMENT & EXTRACTIVE METABOLISM

Crustal Concentration & Non-Fuel Mineral Depletion

Ore Genesis & Static Depletion Horizon

While Earth's continental crust is entirely composed of minerals, less than 0.001% is concentrated into mineable ores by geological processes.

$$\text{EF} = \frac{C_{\text{ore}}}{C_{\text{crust}}} \quad \Longrightarrow \quad t_{\text{exhaustion}} = \frac{R}{P} = \frac{\text{Proven Economic Reserves [t]}}{\text{Annual Production Rate [t/year]}}$$
Extractive Acceleration: Global metals demand increased over 20-fold in the past century (e.g., US demand surged from 160 Mt to 3.3 Gt).
Construction Commodities: Non-fuel mineral mining is driven by civil engineering: Iron ore (67%), Limestone (7%), and Gypsum (6%).
Data Source: USGS Mineral Commodity Summaries & Pacheco-Torgal et al. (2014) Static vs. Dynamic Depletion
GEOLOGICAL LIMITS

Economic vs. Physical Scarcity

Static Paradigm Limit: $R/P$ ignores price dynamics, technological recovery, sub-economic resources, and exponential production growth.
Ore Grade Degradation: Depletion forces extraction of lower-grade deposits, exponentially increasing energy input and refining residues.

MODULE 02 — DYNAMIC RESOURCE DEPLETION

Hubbert Peak Theory & Production Inflexion

Logistic Production Dynamics

Finite mineral exploitation follows a bell-shaped logistic curve where maximum extraction rate occurs when approximately half of recoverable resources are depleted.

$$P(t) = \frac{d Q(t)}{d t} = \frac{k \cdot Q_{\infty} \cdot e^{-k(t - t_{\text{peak}})}}{\left(1 + e^{-k(t - t_{\text{peak}})}\right)^2} \quad \left[\frac{\text{tons}}{\text{year}}\right]$$
Hubbert Peak ($t_{\text{peak}}$): Point of maximum extraction velocity where production rate can no longer keep pace with growing infrastructural demand.
Cumulative Endowment ($Q_{\infty}$): Total integrated area beneath the curve, comprising past production, proven reserves, and undiscovered deposits.
Theoretical Basis: M. King Hubbert (1956); Pacheco-Torgal et al. (2014) Dynamic Depletion Horizon
PROJECTED PEAKS

Peak Timing Across Commodities

Copper Imminent Inflexion: Peak extraction occurs between 2012 and 2068 depending on reserve discovery assumptions, threatening electrical grid deployment.
Structural Aluminum & Iron: Bauxite/Al reaches production peak around 2088; global Iron ore peaks around 2115, after which extraction energy rises exponentially.

MODULE 03 — INTERGENERATIONAL ACCOUNTING & EXERGY DEGRADATION

The Grave-to-Cradle Paradigm & Exergy Costs

Intergenerational Replacement Exergy

Extracting high-concentration ores today forces future generations to exploit lower-grade crustal deposits, escalating required energy and ecological disruption.

$$B_{\text{G2C}} = \int_{x_{\text{ore}}}^{x_{\text{crust}}} \left( \frac{\partial B_{\text{conc}}}{\partial x} \right) dx = T_0 \cdot \Delta S_{\text{mix}} + \Delta H_{\text{benef}} \quad \left[\frac{\text{kJ}}{\text{kg}}\right]$$
From Cradle-to-Grave: Standard LCA quantifies extraction to disposal; it ignores the cost to reconstitute or replace dissipated mineral capital.
To Grave-to-Cradle (G2C): Calculates theoretical exergy needed to reconcentrate degraded tailings back to original natural deposit grades.
Theoretical Architecture: Valero & Valero (Thanatia Hypothesis) Thermodynamic Depletion Debt
PARADIGM EVOLUTION

Fixed-Stock vs. Opportunity Cost

Fixed-Stock View (Physical Mass): Assumes resources simply run out abruptly; fails to capture progressive economic and energetic quality downgrades.
Opportunity Cost View (Exergy Quality): Measures the escalating energy penalty imposed on future societies to recover dispersed construction minerals.

METHODOLOGICAL PARADIGM SHIFT

From Grave to Cradle: Accounting for Mineral Depletion

The Extraction Legacy:

Current industry extracts the highest-purity, easily accessible mineral stocks. Once extracted and dispersed into urban infrastructure and demolition rubble, entropy increases. Reconstituting that chemical capital requires astronomical energy expenditures that standard cradle-to-grave LCA completely omits.

The Grave-to-Cradle Stage:

Proposed by mineral ecological economists, the "Grave-to-Cradle" stage adds a corrective metric: it computes the thermodynamic exergy necessary to return dispersed materials back to their baseline natural state. This penalizes linear dissipation and makes high-quality recycling economically and thermodynamically indispensable.

By incorporating exergy replacement costs, building materials demonstrate up to 300% higher actual ecological burdens.
