MODULE 02 — NORMATIVE FRAMEWORK & ENVIRONMENTAL CHARACTERIZATION

Solid Waste Classification (ABNT NBR 10004)

Class I: Hazardous

Presents intrinsic risks to public health and the environment due to critical physicochemical properties.

  • Flammability, Corrosivity or Reactivity
  • Toxicity or Pathogenicity criteria
  • CNEN governs radioactive waste exclusion
Tap to inspect hazardous mechanisms

Class II: Non-Hazardous

Segregated by degradation behavior and leaching/solubilization resistance into subcategories II A and II B.

  • Class II A (Non-Inert): Biodegradable, combustible, or soluble
  • Class II B (Inert): Stable in water without toxic dissolution
  • Evaluated via ABNT NBR 10006 solubilization

REGULATORY CLASSIFICATION: CLASS I

Hazardous Solid Residues (NBR 10004)

Materials that pose direct threats to human health (morbidity/mortality) or environmental equilibrium due to flammability, corrosivity, reactivity, toxicity (Annex F), or pathogenicity.

Management Mandate: Requires strict chain of custody, manifest tracking, specialized containment, and cannot be co-disposed with ordinary construction debris.
REGULATORY CLASSIFICATION: CLASS II

Non-Hazardous Residues (NBR 10004)

Non-hazardous materials subclassified into Class II A (Non-Inert) exhibiting solubility or biodegradability, and Class II B (Inert) which undergo no chemical transfer above potable standards.

Class II A (Non-Inert): Exceeds Annex G solubilization thresholds (e.g., taste, odor, iron, sulfates) without acute toxicity.
Class II B (Inert): Clears all Annex G potable thresholds; suitable for recycled aggregates in structural elements.

MODULE 03 — LEACHING EXTRACTION PROTOCOLS

Leached Extract Procedure (ABNT NBR 10005)

Mass Transfer & Extract Phase Separation

Simulates leaching under aggressive landfill conditions to differentiate Class I (Hazardous) from Class II through controlled acetic extraction.

$$\% \, \text{Sólidos Secos} = \frac{(m_{\text{resíduo + filtro}} - m_{\text{filtro}})}{m_{\text{inicial do resíduo}}} \times 100$$
Granulometric Cut: Must pass through a $9.5\text{ mm}$ sieve before extraction ($T \le 4^\circ\text{C}$ for volatiles).
Agitation Dynamics: End-over-end agitation ($30 \pm 2\text{ rpm}$) for $18 \pm 2\text{ h}$ at liquid/solid ratio of $20:1$.
Governed by ABNT NBR 10005:2004 Liquid/Solid Ratio: 20:1
EXTRACTION MEDIA CRITERIA

Acid Solution Selection

Solution #1 ($\text{pH } 4.93 \pm 0.05$): Glacial acetic acid ($5.7\text{ mL}$) + $1.0\text{ N NaOH}$ ($64.3\text{ mL}$) made up to $1\text{ L}$. Used if preliminary test $\text{pH} \le 5.0$.
Solution #2 ($\text{pH } 2.88 \pm 0.05$): Glacial acetic acid ($5.7\text{ mL}$) diluted with deionized water to $1\text{ L}$. Applied when preliminary $\text{pH} > 5.0$.
ABNT NBR 10005:2004 — Obtenção de Extrato Lixiviado de Resíduos Sólidos Press ↓ for pH Extraction Lab | → for Solubilization (NBR 10006)
LABORATORY SPECIFICATIONS

NBR 10005 Chemical Reagents & Glassware

Leaching solutions utilize pure analytical grade glacial acetic acid ($\text{CH}_3\text{COOH}$) and $1.0\text{ N NaOH}$ to test residue buffer resistance under acidic percolation.

Filtration Apparatus: Pressurized borosilicate device with fiberglass or nitrocellulose filters ($0.6\text{ to }0.8\,\mu\text{m}$ pore size).
Volatile Extractions (ZHE): Zero Headspace Extractor prevents loss of volatile organics during the 18-hour continuous extraction run.

Regulatory rule: Waste alkalinity overcame acid challenge ($\text{pH} > 5.0$), triggering the more aggressive unbuffered fluid.

MODULE 04 — WATER EXTRACTION & SOLUBILIZATION

Solubilized Extract Protocol (ABNT NBR 10006)

Static Dissolution & Inertness Assessment

Standardized protocol to differentiate Class II A (Non-Inert) from Class II B (Inert) by determining transfer of chemical species into pure deionized water.

Sample Preparation: Dry sample at $\le 42^\circ\text{C}$ in forced-air oven. Granulometric cut passes $9.5\text{ mm}$ sieve.
Mass & Solvent Ratio: $250\text{ g}$ dry residue $+ 1000\text{ mL}$ organic-free deionized water in $1500\text{ mL}$ flask ($4:1$ ratio).
Static Contact Horizon: Covered with PVC film; left undisturbed for 7 days ($168\text{ h}$) at $\le 25^\circ\text{C}$.
Membrane Filtration: Vacuum-filtered through $0.45\,\mu\text{m}$ porosity membrane to extract dissolved ions.
Governed by ABNT NBR 10006:2004 Liquid/Solid Ratio: 4:1 (Static)
REGULATORY CRITERIA

Potability Standards Benchmark

Annex G Drinking Water Limits: Element concentrations in solubilized extract are cross-checked against strict drinking water parameters ($As, Pb, Cr, Fe, Al, Mn, Zn, Cu$).
Classification Threshold: Any element exceeding Annex G automatically classifies residue as Class II A (Non-Inert); compliance with all limits qualifies it as Class II B (Inert).
ABNT NBR 10006:2004 — Procedimento para Obtenção de Extrato Solubilizado de Resíduos Sólidos Press ↓ for Annex G Simulator | → for Lab Apparatus (Slide 5,0)
ABNT NBR 10004:2004

Annex G Standards for Solubilization

The 7-day extraction reveals whether solid waste or byproducts dissolve secondary contaminants, altering potable water parameters like taste, appearance, or trace toxicity.

Heavy Metal Limits: $As \le 0.01\,\text{mg/L}$, $Pb \le 0.01\,\text{mg/L}$, $Cr \le 0.05\,\text{mg/L}$, $Cu \le 2.0\,\text{mg/L}$.
Secondary Cation Limits: $Fe \le 0.3\,\text{mg/L}$, $Al \le 0.2\,\text{mg/L}$, $Mn \le 0.1\,\text{mg/L}$, $Zn \le 5.0\,\text{mg/L}$.

MODULE 05 — EXPERIMENTAL APPARATUS & INSTRUMENTATION

Laboratory Hardware & Glassware Protocols

Agitation & Conditioning

Calibrated mechanical equipment ensuring boundary layer mass transfer and steady temperature kinetics.

  • Rotary Agitator: End-over-end dynamic tumbler ($30 \pm 2\text{ rpm}$).
  • Forced-Air Oven: Sample drying at $\le 42^\circ\text{C}$ without volatile loss.
  • Precision Balance: $\pm 0.01\text{ g}$ resolution gravimetric measurement.
Inspect mechanical tolerances

Separation & Filtration

Pressure devices and calibrated porous membranes preventing fine particulate contamination in leachates.

  • Pressurized Filter Holder: Positive pressure up to $350\text{ kPa}$.
  • $0.45\,\mu\text{m}$ Membrane: Distinguishes true dissolved ions from colloids.
  • Büchner & Kitasato Assembly: Rapid vacuum-assisted clarification.
Inspect membrane specs
TRACE PURITY

Analytical Instrumentation

Inert glassware, automated sensors, and zero-headspace vessels preventing environmental bias.

  • Digital pH Meter: Multi-point slope calibration ($\pm 0.05\text{ units}$).
  • Borosilicate 3.3 Flasks: Alkali-leaching resistant chemical bodies.
  • Zero-Headspace (ZHE): Piston vessel preventing volatile escape.
Inspect sensor & vessel protocols
ABNT NBR 10005 / NBR 10006 Hardware Standard Requirements Press → for Leaching Kinetics (Slide 6,0)
EQUIPMENT STANDARD

Rotary Tumbler & Drying Ovens

End-over-end rotational tumbler prevents dead-zone sedimentation during the 18-hour continuous extraction. Forced aeration ovens preserve thermally unstable contaminants.

Kinetic Speed: Calibrated strictly to $30 \pm 2\text{ rpm}$ to maintain liquid-solid interfacial equilibrium without attrition.
Thermal Cap: Drying temperatures must never exceed $42^\circ\text{C}$ to avoid volatile stripping or pyrolytic transformations.
FILTRATION ARCHITECTURE

Membrane Selection & Pressure Systems

Filtration isolates true chemical species in solution from suspended sub-micrometer mineral particulates that would artificially distort leachate spectrometry.

NBR 10005 Cut-Off: Borosilicate fiber or PTFE membranes with nominal pore diameters between $0.6\,\mu\text{m}$ and $0.8\,\mu\text{m}$.
NBR 10006 Cut-Off: Strict hydrophilic cellulose ester membranes with $0.45\,\mu\text{m}$ pore rating for drinking water analogies.
TRACE CONTAINMENT

Zero-Headspace Extraction & Potentiometry

Specialized Zero-Headspace Extractor (ZHE) systems completely eliminate the air-liquid interface to contain volatile organic compounds (VOCs) during rigorous mechanical tumbling.

Pre-Cooling Requirement: When testing for volatiles, residues must be cooled to $4^\circ\text{C}$ prior to milling to preserve BTEX and chlorinated solvents.
MODULE 06 — MASS TRANSFER & TOXICOLOGICAL RISK

Leaching Dynamics & Ecotoxicity Testing

Leaching Governing Factors

Mass transfer rate is dictated by boundary layer equilibrium and interfacial exposure variables.

  • Leaching Agent: Deionized water vs. acetic acid buffer.
  • Particle Size ($< 9.5\text{ mm}$): Specific surface controls ionic flux.
  • Agitation & L/S Ratio: Hydrodynamic regime and cumulative stage extractions ($L/S = 20:1$).
Inspect transfer mechanisms

Immobilization in Composites

Residue safety verification in blended cements, geopolymer precursors, and concrete matrices.

  • Alkaline Buffer: Composite matrix neutralizes acid percolation agents.
  • Hydration Trapping: Heavy metals locked in C-S-H / ettringite phases.
  • Standard Inconsistency: Lack of realistic monolithic protocols raises industry confusion.
Inspect chemical fixation
BIOASSAYS VS CHEMISTRY

Ecotoxicity & Bio-Reactivity

Bulk chemical quantification does not predict real bio-availability, chronic eco-toxicity, or cellular synergies.

  • Bioassays: Standard toxicity testing using Daphnia magna, microalgae, and bioluminescent bacteria.
  • Cocktail Effect: Synergistic toxicity of multiple trace heavy metals under variable pH.
  • Bio-accumulation: Environmental migration pathways in soils and surface waters.
Inspect bioassay criteria

MASS TRANSFER PHENOMENOLOGY

Leaching Kinetic Variables

Leaching is governed by diffusion, surface dissolution, and chemical complexation. Accelerated batch tests force high surface-to-volume exposure that may exaggerate unconfined environmental release.

Particle Granulometry: Milling materials below $9.5\text{ mm}$ (or $4\text{ mm}$ in EN 12457) breaks protective mineral crusts, exposing encapsulated phases to immediate attack.
Extraction Fluid: Glacial acetic acid simulates municipal solid waste codisposal, while deionized water reflects real-world meteoric rainwater leaching.
SOLIDIFICATION / STABILIZATION (S/S)

Immobilization Mechanisms in Binders

Hydraulic cement matrices chemically encapsulate toxic ions within stable crystalline lattices and physically isolate contaminants behind dense, low-permeability hydration products.

Buffering Dynamics: When crushed concrete is subjected to acid extractors, Portlandite ($\text{Ca(OH)}_2$) buffers the acid solution, elevating the pH and precipitating heavy metals as insoluble hydroxides.
BIOLOGICAL RISK EVALUATION

Ecotoxicological Assays vs Chemical Spectra

Comparing leachate element concentrations to regulatory thresholds is insufficient to rule out toxicity because speciation, bioavailability, and antagonistic/synergistic cellular reactions govern true ecological damage.

Organism Responses: Tests on indicator species evaluate acute mortality ($LC_{50}$), enzyme inhibition, and chronic reproductive disruption from minute leachate traces.
Composite Compatibilization: Modern sustainable construction research requires coupling standard NBR 10005/6 chemistry with microbiological screening.

METHODOLOGICAL CONVERGENCE

Acid Extraction vs Rainwater Leaching

EPA 1311 & NBR 10005: Designed for municipal landfill co-disposal simulation (fermenting municipal solid waste producing organic acids). Highly aggressive.
EN 12457 & DIN 38414: Utilizes pure water to simulate real atmospheric percolation on road embankments and unconfined recycled aggregates.
Comparative Normative Evaluation: ABNT vs. US EPA vs. CEN European Norms Press → for Case Study: Ore Tailings vs Commercial (Slide 8,0)
STANDARDIZATION CHALLENGES

Compatibility Gaps Across Jurisdictions

Differences in particle crushing ($4\text{ mm}$ vs $9.5\text{ mm}$), leachant chemistry, and cumulative L/S ratios cause conflicting classifications for the exact same industrial residue.

Monolith vs Granular: Crushing monolithic concrete into fine grains destroys the physical immobilization matrix, overestimating real-world leaching rates.
European Two-Stage Test: EN 12457-3 evaluates wash-off kinetics at $L/S = 2\,\text{l/kg}$ followed by slow diffusion at cumulative $L/S = 10\,\text{l/kg}$.

ANALYTICAL DIAGNOSIS

Immobilization vs. Baseline Raw Materials

Leaching Compliance (Class I Rule-Out): All toxic heavy metals ($As, Pb, Cr$) leached under NBR 10005 remain orders of magnitude below Annex F and US EPA caps ($As = 0.0142 \ll 1.0\text{ mg/L}$). Neither block is hazardous.
Solubilization Paradox: Commercial blocks leach more $Al$ ($0.4585\text{ mg/L}$) and $As$ ($0.0594\text{ mg/L}$) than IOT blocks, proving natural raw aggregates carry intrinsic soluble phases.

CASE STUDY INSIGHTS

Iron Ore Tailings vs Commercial Aggregates

Cementitious hydration binds tailing fines into crystalline C-S-H gels, delivering lower solubilized release of aluminum ($0.0943$ vs $0.4585\text{ mg/L}$) than traditional natural river sand mixtures.

Chemical Fixation: Iron oxides and heavy metalloids present in tailings enter stable non-reactive phases upon alkaline hydration.
Annex G Standard Limit: Even standard commercial concrete fails pure drinking water potability requirements due to trace ionic background.
