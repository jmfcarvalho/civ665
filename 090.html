MODULE 01 — MACROECONOMIC MATERIAL DYNAMICS

Anthropogenic Acceleration & Planetary Limits

The Fixed-Stock Paradigm (1972–Present)

The industrial metabolism of modern construction drives exponential resource extraction, pushing the ecosphere beyond safe planetary boundaries.

$$M(t) = M_0 \cdot e^{k \cdot t} \quad \Longrightarrow \quad \Delta S_{\text{crust}} = \int_0^T \sum \dot{m}_{i,\text{extracted}}(t)\,dt \quad [\text{Gt}]$$
8-Fold Surge Last Century: Raw material throughput grew eightfold in the 20th century and is on track to double again by 2050.
Construction Dominance (40%): Built infrastructure consumes 40% of all extracted virgin materials, generating 25% of solid waste.
Foundations: Meadows et al. (Limits to Growth, 1972) Structural Decoupling Imperative
CIVIL FOOTPRINT

Macro Burden Partitioning

Primary Energy & Water: Buildings and construction account for 40% of global primary energy and 15% of all accessible freshwater.
GHG Emissions Share: Direct and indirect manufacturing emissions generate between 40% and 50% of worldwide greenhouse gases.

Construction Industry Share of Global Resources

Global Physical Consumption:
  • Raw Materials: 40% of all mineral and non-fuel geological flows.
  • Primary Energy: 40% consumed directly in fabrication and operations.
  • Freshwater: 15% dedicated to processing, batching, and curing.

Evolution of Environmental Scrutiny:

Early conservation concerns focused strictly on local toxicity. In 1972, the Club of Rome model warned of societal exhaustion within the 21st century. The first comprehensive Life Cycle Assessment applied to structural concrete emerged only in 2012, highlighting a four-decade lag in holistic environmental accounting for bulk infrastructure.

Material efficiency and secondary pozzolans are mandatory to avoid the 2050 demand doubling penalty.

MODULE 02 — STANDARDIZATION & BOUNDARY PARADIGMS

LCA Frameworks, Standards & Environmental Labels

Standardized 4-Stage Architecture

Standardized LCA tracks inputs, energetic transformations, and emissions across defined life cycle stages (ISO 14040/44).

$$\text{LCI} = \sum_{j} m_j \cdot c_{i,j} \quad \Longrightarrow \quad \text{LCIA}_k = \sum_{i} \text{LCI}_i \cdot \text{CF}_{i,k} \quad [\text{Impact units}]$$
Cradle-to-Gate (A1–A3): Covers raw extraction and processing; the most critical manufacturing phase for concrete.
Cradle-to-Grave (A1–C4): Encompasses logistics (A4), installation (A5), use/maintenance (B), and final demolition/disposal (C).
Normative Standards: ISO 14040/44, ISO 14025, ISO 21930 Methodological Harmonization
LABELING HIERARCHY

EPDs & Recycling Debates

Type III Declarations (EPDs): Third-party verified parameter declarations governed by ISO 14025 and refined by ISO 21930 for construction goods.
The Allocation Dilemma: Where are recycling benefits credited? To the primary material producer or to the secondary downcycling user?

NORMATIVE CLASSIFICATION

Environmental Declarations & Labeling Types (ISO)

Type I (ISO 14024):

Voluntary, multi-criteria, third-party verified eco-labels (e.g., German Blue Angel, 1978). Awards certification based on compliance with preset environmental performance thresholds.

Type II (ISO 14021):

Self-declared environmental claims made directly by manufacturers without mandatory third-party verification. Historically prone to greenwashing suspicions.

Type III (ISO 14025):

Environmental Product Declarations (EPDs). Quantifies lifecycle environmental performance via predetermined indicators without imposing arbitrary pass/fail scores.

EPDs are strictly governed by Product Category Rules (PCRs) under ISO 21930 and EN 15804.

MODULE 03 — MULTI-CRITERIA DECISION ANALYSIS (MCDA)

Composite Eco-Efficiency & Desirability Functions

Derringer-Suich Mathematical Formulation

Normalizes disparate multi-category LCA indicators relative to structural performance, eliminating subjective weighting controversy.

$$d_i(y) = \left[ \frac{y_{\max} - y}{y_{\max} - y_{\min}} \right]^w \quad \Longrightarrow \quad \text{EEP} = \left( \prod_{k=1}^{5} I_k \right)^{1/5}$$
Compressive Normalization ($f_c$): Impacts are divided by strength ($I_j / f_c$) to prevent allocating false environmental superiority to structurally underperforming mixtures.
Geometric Mean Aggregation: If any individual category collapses to zero desirability ($d_i = 0$), the overall Eco-Efficiency Potential ($\text{EEP}$) collapses to zero.
Analytical Formulation: Souza et al. (2021) — J. Build. Eng. 10-Category Hierarchical Model
PILLAR TAXONOMY

The 5 Core Indicator Groups

Energy & Carbon: $\text{EIR}$ (Total Primary Energy reduction) and $\text{GGR}$ (Global Warming Potential abatement).
Ecosystem Protection: $\text{MIR}$ (Raw Materials & Water), $\text{EPR}$ (Eco-Toxicity), and $\text{AEO}$ (Acidification, Eutrophication, Ozone).

HIERARCHICAL INDICATORS (SOUZA ET AL., 2021)

10 Midpoint Categories to 5 Eco-Efficiency Indices

1. EIR (Energy): Total Consumption of Primary Energy ($\text{TPE} / f_c$).
2. MIR (Materials): Primary Raw Material ($\text{PRM}$), Recycled Material ($\text{RM}$), and Water Consumption ($\text{WC}$).
3. GGR (Greenhouse): Global Warming Potential ($\text{GWP} / f_c$).
4. EPR (Ecotoxicity): Freshwater ($\text{FAETP}$), Terrestrial ($\text{TETP}$), and Marine ($\text{MAETP}$) Ecotoxicity.
5. AEO (Abatement): Acidification ($\text{AP}$), Eutrophication ($\text{EP}$), and Ozone Depletion ($\text{ODP}$).
Target Scaling: Individual parameters mapped between worst-case ($d=0.0$) and best-practice target ($d=1.0$).
Overall Eco-Efficiency Potential: $\text{EEP} = (\text{EIR} \times \text{MIR} \times \text{GGR} \times \text{EPR} \times \text{AEO})^{1/5}$.
