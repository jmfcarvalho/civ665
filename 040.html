Resource Depletion & Mining Overburden

The Extraction Waste-to-Ore Disparity

Global Mass Inefficiency
6,000 Mt
Residues and overburden generated to obtain only 900 Mt of usable mineral raw material.
Tailings & Slurry Accumulation: Overburden removal and hydrometallurgical processing yield colossal sterile stockpiles requiring long-term containment.
Ecosystem Demolition: Massive topsoil stripping, water table contamination, acid mine drainage (AMD), and local biodiversity extinction.
Catastrophic Tailings Dam Failures: Structural collapse of upstream wet tailings dams poses irreversible socio-environmental damage.
CRITICAL QUESTIONS

Depletion of Non-Renewables

Are contemporary extraction velocities structurally outstripping geological recovery and discoverable economic reserves?

  • • Non-Renewable Baseline: Mineral ores are fixed natural capital stocks.
  • • Declining Ore Grades: Lower concentrations demand exponentially higher specific energy for extraction and comminution.
  • • Spatial Inequity: Concentrated geographic reserves generate severe supply-chain geopolitical vulnerability.
Extraction risk is not merely physical depletion, but exponential environmental degradation per kilogram refined.

Forensic Case Study

Fundão Tailings Dam Failure (Mariana, 2015)

The breach of the Fundão tailings dam (Samarco/Vale/BHP) released approximately 39.2 to 43.7 million m³ of iron ore tailings slurry. The disaster leveled the sub-district of Bento Rodrigues, claimed 19 lives, and traveled over 650 km along the Doce River basin to the Atlantic Ocean. It remains one of the largest environmental mining disasters in history, triggered primarily by static liquefaction in an upstream-raised embankment structure.

Full Case Analysis Source: Technical Forensics / Wikipedia
Forensic Case Study

Córrego do Feijão Dam Failure (Brumadinho, 2019)

The catastrophic collapse of Dam B1 (Vale S.A.) released roughly 12 million m³ of tailings via sudden static liquefaction with virtually zero warning time for downstream facilities. The failure claimed 272 confirmed lives and severely impacted the Paraopeba River ecosystem. The disaster triggered rigorous federal regulatory changes (Brazilian Federal Law 14,066/2020), mandating the total decommissioning and banning of upstream-raised tailings dams nationwide.
