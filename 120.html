MODULE 01 — GHG PROTOCOL & PROCESS DECARBONIZATION

Carbon Footprint & Scope 1–3 Partitioning

Stoichiometric & Energy Emission Vectors

Decomposes structural concrete emissions into direct chemical calcination, thermal fuel combustion, electrical grids, and logistics chains.

$$\text{GWP}_{\text{total}} = \underbrace{m_{\text{clinker}} \cdot (\alpha_{\text{calc}} + \beta_{\text{fuel}})}_{\text{Scope 1 (Direct)}} + \underbrace{E_{\text{elec}} \cdot \text{EF}_{\text{grid}}}_{\text{Scope 2 (Indirect Grid)}} + \underbrace{\sum_{k} m_k \cdot d_k \cdot \text{EF}_{\text{trans},k}}_{\text{Scope 3 (Upstream / Logistics)}}$$
Scope 1 Calcination ($\alpha_{\text{calc}}$): Stoichiometric decarbonation ($\text{CaCO}_3 \xrightarrow{\Delta} \text{CaO} + \text{CO}_2$) emits exactly $0.785\ \text{kg CO}_2/\text{kg CaO}$ ($\sim 525\ \text{kg CO}_2/\text{t clinker}$).
Scopes 2 & 3 Drivers: Grinding/milling electricity accounts for Scope 2 ($E_{\text{elec}} \sim 90\text{--}130\ \text{kWh/t}$), while heavy raw mineral freight dominates Scope 3.
Normative Framework: GHG Protocol Corporate Standard & ISO 14064-1 Process Emission Accounting
CARBON LEDGER

Clinker Factor & Fuel Switching

Clinker-to-Cement Ratio: Reducing clinker factor from 0.75 to 0.50 via supplementary cementitious materials (SCMs) directly scales down raw limestone calcination.
Alternative Fuel Coprocessing: Replacing petroleum coke with biomass residues and non-recyclable refuse-derived fuel (RDF) mitigates Scope 1 thermal emissions ($\beta_{\text{fuel}}$).

GHG PROTOCOL & ISO 14064 TAXONOMY

Scopes 1, 2 and 3 in the Cementitious Supply Chain

Scope 1 (Direct):
  • Process Calcination: $\approx 60\text{--}65\%$ of clinker emissions, chemically released from limestone.
  • Stationary Combustion: Rotary kiln fuel burn ($\approx 30\text{--}35\%$ of clinker footprint).
  • Quarry Fleet: Heavy mobile excavation machinery operating on-site.
Scope 2 (Indirect Energy):
  • Raw Meal Grinding: High-pressure vertical roller mills.
  • Clinker Finishing: Ball mills for clinker-gypsum grinding.
  • Batching Plant: Concrete plant mixers, pneumatic silos, and aggregate conveyors.
Scope 3 (Supply Chain):
  • Raw Aggregates Extraction: Natural sand and gravel crushing.
  • Inbound Logistics: Fuel consumed hauling SCMs, aggregates, and admixtures to the ready-mix batch plant.
  • Outbound Delivery: Transit mixer trucks to construction site.
In structural concrete, Scope 1 clinker calcination constitutes over 70% of the cradle-to-gate carbon footprint.

MODULE 02 — ISO 14045 & INTENSITY METRICS

Eco-Efficiency Ratios & Binder Indices

Quantitative Structural Normalization

Links structural load-bearing performance ($f_c$) and service life directly to lifecycle carbon emissions, avoiding false low-strength eco-claims.

$$\text{EER} = \frac{\text{Product Value}}{\text{Environmental Impact}} \quad \Longleftrightarrow \quad \text{bi} = \frac{b}{f_c} \quad \left[\frac{\text{kg binder}}{\text{m}^3 \cdot \text{MPa}}\right] \quad \text{ci} = \frac{\text{GWP}}{f_c} \quad \left[\frac{\text{kg CO}_2\text{e}}{\text{m}^3 \cdot \text{MPa}}\right]$$
Binder Intensity ($\text{bi}$): Measures binder consumption per unit of compressive strength delivered ($b/f_c$). World benchmark targets $\text{bi} \le 5.0\ \text{kg}/(\text{m}^3 \cdot \text{MPa})$.
CO₂ Intensity ($\text{ci}$): Normalizes cradle-to-gate global warming potential by compressive resistance. World average hovers at $\sim 8.5\ \text{kg CO}_2\text{e}/(\text{m}^3 \cdot \text{MPa})$.
Normative Basis: ISO 14045:2012 & Damineli et al. (2010, Cem. Concr. Res.) Structural Efficiency Indices
ECO-EFFICIENCY BENCHMARKS

The Packing & Clinker Nexus

Particle Packing Decoupling: Optimizing aggregate grain size distributions decreases intergranular porosity, reducing required paste volume and suppressing $\text{bi}$.
Clinker Intensity ($\text{cli}$): Replaces clinker with pozzolans, slag, or fillers to decouple structural load performance from limestone calcination emissions.

MODULE 03 — MULTI-OBJECTIVE TRADE-OFF OPTIMIZATION

Pareto Optimization & Low-Carbon Mix Design

Non-Dominated Solutions Vector

Resolves non-trivial structural conflicts between life cycle global warming potential, unit economic production cost, and mechanical durability.

$$\min_{\mathbf{x}} \mathbf{F}(\mathbf{x}) = \begin{bmatrix} \text{GWP}(\mathbf{x}) \\ \text{Cost}(\mathbf{x}) \end{bmatrix} \quad \text{s.t.} \quad \begin{cases} f_c(\mathbf{x}) \ge f_{c,\text{target}} \\ D_{\text{app}}(\mathbf{x}) \le D_{\text{limit}} \\ \sum x_i = 1.0\ \text{m}^3 \end{cases}$$
Pareto Dominance ($\mathbf{x}_1 \prec \mathbf{x}_2$): A concrete mix dominates another if it delivers strictly lower GWP and cost without sacrificing structural compressive resistance or durability.
Economic Marginal Abatement: Quantifies the marginal financial investment required ($\Delta \$ / \Delta \text{t CO}_2\text{e}$) when transitioning from OPC to ternary calcined-clay systems.
Analytical Formulation: Habert et al. (2020) & ISO 14045:2012 Non-Dominated Pareto Envelope
TRADE-OFF DYNAMICS

Cost vs. Carbon Abatement

The Early Strength Barrier: High-volume SCM replacements (e.g. >50% GGBS or Fly Ash) exhibit slower early hydration kinetics, requiring chemical activators or thermal curing.
Ternary Synergy Optimization: Combining limestone calcined clay (LC3) with multimodal superplasticizers unlocks optimal Pareto positions with zero cost penalty.

MULTI-CRITERIA DECISION ANALYSIS (MCDA)

Pareto Efficiency & Marginal Decarbonization Abatement

Boundary Envelopes:

Concrete formulation is intrinsically multi-attribute. Increasing clinker substitution with SCMs sharply curtails embodied $\text{CO}_2\text{e}$ emissions, but can induce latency in 1-day stripping strength and demands specialized chemical admixtures that shift material pricing. Solutions on the Pareto envelope represent mixtures where no single objective can be enhanced without degrading another.

Marginal Abatement Metric:

The marginal abatement cost curve ($\text{MACC}$) measures: $$\text{MAC} = \frac{\text{Cost}_{\text{green}} - \text{Cost}_{\text{ref}}}{\text{GWP}_{\text{ref}} - \text{GWP}_{\text{green}}} \quad \left[\frac{\text{\$}}{\text{t CO}_2\text{e avoided}}\right]$$ Industrial by-products such as blast furnace slag and low-grade fly ash often exhibit negative or neutral abatement costs when aggregate packing is optimized.

Pareto optimality eliminates arbitrary single-score weighting bias in structural concrete procurement.
