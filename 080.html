MODULE 01 — ENVELOPE THERMODYNAMICS & INSULATION CRITERIA

Thermal Insulation Foundations & Conduction Physics

Regulatory Thresholds & Fourier Transfer

Materials specified to attenuate building operational enthalpy dissipation must verify strict conduction and resistance bounds.

$$q = -k \nabla T \quad \Longleftrightarrow \quad U = \frac{1}{R_{\text{se}} + \sum \frac{d_i}{\lambda_i} + R_{\text{si}}} \quad \left[\frac{\text{W}}{\text{m}^2\cdot\text{K}}\right]$$
Insulating Criterion: Thermal conductivity $\lambda \le 0.065\text{ W/(m}\cdot\text{K)}$ and minimal thermal resistance $R \ge 0.30\text{ m}^2\text{K/W}$.
Surface Film Resistances: Standardized environmental boundaries account for internal convective film $R_{\text{si}}$ and exterior wind exposure $R_{\text{se}}$.
Data Source: Santos & Matias (2006); EN ISO 6946 Operational Decarbonization Core
CONDUCTIVITY SPECTRUM

Envelope Conduction Contrast

Heavy Structural Solids ($\lambda > 1.0\text{ W/m}\cdot\text{K}$): Current concrete ($\lambda \approx 2.0$) and structural steel ($\lambda \approx 50.0$) create massive thermal short-circuits.
Porous Trapped-Gas Matrices ($\lambda < 0.045\text{ W/m}\cdot\text{K}$): Foamed polymers (EPS/XPS), mineral wool, and expanded cork impede molecular vibrational transfer.

MODULE 02 — INSULANT TAXONOMY & EMBODIED PENALTIES

Conventional Petrochemical vs. Bio-Based Insulants

The Production Energy Disparity

Petrochemical foamed polymers provide thermal resistance at the cost of high upstream fossil enthalpy during synthesis.

$$\text{EPBT} = \frac{E_{\text{embodied}}}{E_{\text{saved, annual}}} = \frac{\rho \cdot d \cdot EE_m}{\Delta U \cdot \text{HDD} \cdot 24 \cdot 3.6 \times 10^{-3}} \quad [\text{years}]$$
Synthetic Polymers: EPS ($31\text{ kWh/kg}$) and XPS ($28\text{ kWh/kg}$) require massive fossil cracking and polymerization energy.
Bio & Natural Binders: Expanded cork ($1.1\text{ kWh/kg}$) and stone wool ($5.7\text{ kWh/kg}$) cut primary energy investments by up to 96%.
Data Source: Santos & Matias (2006); Berge (2009) Lifecycle Energy Payback Paradigm
HYGROTHERMAL BALANCE

Natural Fibers & Degradation

Renewable Cellular Structure: Trapped air inside plant tissues or expanded cork suberin provides $\lambda = 0.040\text{--}0.045\text{ W/m}\cdot\text{K}$.
Biological Vulnerability: Moisture absorption induces fungal decay; inorganic composite encasement (plaster, cement) is mandatory.

Manufacturing Energy of Thermal Insulants

Specific Production Energy ($kWh/kg$):
  • Expanded Cork Board (ICB): $\approx 1.1\text{ kWh/kg}$
  • Mineral Rock / Glass Wool: $\approx 5.7\text{ kWh/kg}$
  • Rigid Polyurethane Foam (PUR): $\approx 19.5\text{ kWh/kg}$
  • Extruded Polystyrene (XPS): $\approx 27.8\text{ kWh/kg}$
  • Molded Expanded Polystyrene (EPS): $\approx 31.0\text{ kWh/kg}$
Volumetric Density Trade-Off:

While polymers have very high gravimetric energy ($kWh/kg$), their low apparent density ($\rho = 20\text{--}35\text{ kg/m}^3$) partly dampens volumetric embodied energy ($MJ/m^3$). Conversely, cork requires higher mass ($\approx 110\text{ kg/m}^3$) but its renewable origin yields a net-negative biogenic carbon baseline.

Thermal conversion factor: $1\text{ kWh} = 3.6\text{ MJ} = 860\text{ kcal}$.

MODULE 03 — SUPER-INSULATION & VACUUM SYSTEMS

Vacuum Insulation Panels (VIPs) & Gas Fillers

Knudsen Suppression & Edge Bridging

Eliminating molecular gas collisions drops conductivity to $\lambda < 0.005\text{ W/(m}\cdot\text{K)}$, but metallic barrier envelopes induce perimeter thermal bridges.

$$U_{\text{eff}} = U_{\text{cop}} + \psi_{\text{edge}} \cdot \frac{P}{A} = \frac{\lambda_{\text{cop}}}{d} + \psi_{\text{edge}} \cdot \frac{2(w+h)}{w \cdot h} \quad \left[\frac{\text{W}}{\text{m}^2\cdot\text{K}}\right]$$
Center-of-Panel ($\lambda_{\text{cop}}$): Fumed silica / glass fiber under vacuum ($0.002\text{--}0.005\text{ W/m}\cdot\text{K}$) achieves extreme resistance in minimal thickness.
Linear Bridge Penalty ($\psi$): Multilayer aluminum barrier foils channel heat laterally, penalizing small-format panel arrays.
Data Source: Santos & Matias (2006); Berge (2009) High-Performance Minimal Profile
GAS FILLERS & AEROGELS

High-Performance Spectrum

Heavy Inert Gases: Argon ($0.018$), Krypton ($0.010$), and Xenon ($0.007\text{ W/m}\cdot\text{K}$) suppress conduction across multi-pane glazing cavities.
Brittleness & Puncture: VIPs cannot be cut on-site; perforation vents the vacuum, escalating conductivity up to conventional mineral foam levels ($>0.020$).

MODULE 04 — LATENT HEAT STORAGE & THERMAL INERTIA

Phase Change Materials (PCMs) & Peak Shaving

Solid-Liquid Transitions & Enthalpy Buffer

Materials that exploit isothermal phase transitions to store and release latent enthalpy, decoupling interior comfort from ambient temperature swings.

$$Q_{\text{pcm}} = m \left[ \int_{T_1}^{T_m} c_{p,s}(T)\,dT + \Delta H_m + \int_{T_m}^{T_2} c_{p,l}(T)\,dT \right] \quad [\text{kJ}]$$
Isothermal Storage ($\Delta H_m$): High fusion enthalpy ($120\text{--}300\text{ kJ/kg}$) maintains surface temperature near $T_m$ during phase change.
Diurnal Peak Shaving: Absorbs daytime solar heat loads and dissipates energy via night ventilation, cutting cooling power demand.
Data Source: Tyagi & Buddhi (2007) Passive Thermal Mass Decoupling
THERMAL CHEMISTRY

Organic Paraffins vs. Salt Hydrates

Organic Paraffins & Fatty Acids ($T_m \approx 20\text{--}28^\circ\text{C}$): Congruent melting, negligible supercooling, non-corrosive, but low conductivity ($\lambda \approx 0.2\text{ W/m}\cdot\text{K}$) and flammable.
Inorganic Hydrated Salts ($\text{CaCl}_2\cdot 6\text{H}_2\text{O}, \text{Na}_2\text{SO}_4\cdot 10\text{H}_2\text{O}$): Superior volumetric latent heat ($\Delta H_m > 200\text{ kJ/kg}$), non-flammable, but prone to phase segregation.

MODULE 05 — MICROSTRUCTURAL THERMOPHYSICS & NON-DESTRUCTIVE TESTING

Cement Composites, Pore Morphology & UPV

Pore Topology & Acoustic-Thermal Coupling

Thermal transport in cementitious matrices is governed by solid-skeleton tortuosity, air void entrapment, and bulk dry density.

$$\lambda = \alpha \cdot e^{\beta \cdot \rho} \quad \Longleftrightarrow \quad \lambda = a \cdot V_p^b \quad \left[\frac{\text{W}}{\text{m}\cdot\text{K}}\right]$$
Non-Destructive UPV ($V_p$): Ultrasonic Pulse Velocity reflects internal solid percolation pathways, enabling real-time thermal conductivity estimation.
Density & Residue Modification: Industrial tailing fillers and air-entrainment suppress solid conduction ($\lambda \approx 2.0 \to 0.25\text{ W/m}\cdot\text{K}$).
Sources: Mendes et al. (2019, 2020) Microstructure-Informed Modeling
EXPERIMENTAL ARTICLES

Density-Velocity Correlations

Conventional Mortars ($V_p > 3800\text{ m/s}$): Denser packing accelerates phonon lattice vibration, driving conductivity above $1.40\text{ W/(m}\cdot\text{K)}$.
Cellular & Residue Mortars ($V_p < 2200\text{ m/s}$): Pore discontinuities scatter acoustic waves and suppress heat flux down to thermal screed levels.
